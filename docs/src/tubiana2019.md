## What the figure shows

The figure summarizes the "main features of lattice-protein sequence alignments" that the
paper then models with restricted Boltzmann machines. The data are an alignment of
sequences that fold specifically into structure ``S_A`` ([`CONTACT_MAP_A`](@ref)), that is,
with folding probability ``P_\text{nat}(S_A \mid v) > 0.995``. Following Jacquin et al.
(2016), the sequences are generated by Metropolis Monte Carlo at inverse temperature
``\beta = 1000`` on the effective energy ``-\log P_\text{nat}``, keeping one sequence every
1,000 steps.

- **(a), (b)** Structure ``S_A`` and a *competing* structure ``S_G``, in which residues 1 and
  26 are neighbours. The paper does not say which structure ``S_G`` is; we use structure
  6863 of the reference set, which is the most frequent runner-up fold of the alignment's
  sequences and has that contact. Chain bonds are drawn solid, contacts dotted.
- **(c)** A subset of the sampled sequences, coloured by amino acid class.
- **(d)** Position weight matrix: at each site the total height is the conservation score
  ``\log 20 + \sum_v f_i(v) \log f_i(v)`` and each letter is proportional to its frequency
  ``f_i(v)``. Sites are either non-conserved or partly conserved; the most conserved sites
  (1, 18, 24, 25) carry charged residues forming salt bridges, or hydrophobic residues in
  the core.
- **(e)** First principal component of the one-hot encoded alignment. It is dominated by the
  *signs* of the charges on a few pairs of contacting sites (for example 1–18, 2–20, 3–26):
  the alignment splits into two subfamilies with opposite charge patterns, which the
  paper relates to the "prototype" versus "compositional" representations learnt by RBMs.
- **(f)** Average covariance between amino acids ``v, v'`` on pairs of sites in contact in
  ``S_A``, ``C(v, v') = \frac{1}{28}\sum_{ij} c^{(S_A)}_{ij}\,[f_{ij}(v, v') - f_i(v) f_j(v')]``.
  The largest entry is cysteine–cysteine, followed by positive covariances between opposite
  charges (E–K, E–R, D–K, D–R) and negative ones between like charges, reflecting the
  Miyazawa–Jernigan interactions through which amino acids interact.
- **(g)** Histogram of the projections of the sequences on the first principal component,
  showing the two subfamilies.

## Sampling protocol

Both versions below were sampled with [`metropolis!`](@ref) at ``\beta = 1000``, keeping one
sequence every 1,000 steps after a burn-in of 10,000 steps, exactly as in the paper, and
then keeping the sequences with ``P_\text{nat} > 0.995``. One difference matters in
practice: at ``\beta = 1000`` a chain almost never crosses between the two subfamilies of
panel (g) (doing so requires passing through sequences with lower ``P_\text{nat}``), so a
single chain samples essentially one subfamily. The proportion of the two subfamilies in
the original alignment is recovered only by running several independent chains. The
reference alignment of Jacquin et al. (2016) shipped with the package
([`Hugo_MSA`](@ref)`(:A)`) is used as the ground truth for the published model.

## Version 1: against the subset of 10,000 structures

This is the model of the paper: `structures = SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES`. The alignment was
sampled with 4 chains of 9,000 sequences each (36,000 sequences, as in the paper).

![Fig. 4 reproduced against the subset of 10,000 structures](assets/tubiana2019_fig4_subset.jpg)

Agreement with the reference alignment of Jacquin et al. (sequences with
``P_\text{nat} > 0.995`` in both cases):

| statistic | fresh sample vs. reference alignment |
|:--|:--|
| site frequencies ``f_i(v)`` | Pearson ``r = 0.997`` |
| conservation profile (panel d) | Pearson ``r = 0.999`` |
| first principal component (panel e) | ``\lvert\cos\rvert = 0.998`` |
| contact covariance ``C(v, v')`` (panel f) | Pearson ``r = 0.9995`` |
| mean ``P_\text{nat}`` | ``0.9964`` in both |

The only visible difference is the relative weight of the two subfamilies in panel (g)
(60/40 here against 43/57 in the reference), which, as explained above, is set by the
basins the 4 chains fell into rather than by the model.

## Version 2: against all 103,346 compact structures

Same protocol with the default structure set, `structures = STRUCTURES`, sampled with
20 chains of 100 sequences each. This is a stricter model: ``P_\text{nat}`` is now computed
against ten times more competitors, so the sequences must fold specifically against all of
them.

![Fig. 4 reproduced against all 103,346 compact structures](assets/tubiana2019_fig4_all.jpg)

The paper's criterion ``P_\text{nat} > 0.995`` is not reached with this protocol under
the full model. At ``\beta = 1000`` the sampled sequences have a mean ``P_\text{nat}`` of
0.984 (median 0.988, 95th percentile 0.993), still slowly increasing after 100,000 steps,
and only 2 of the 2,000 sequences exceed 0.995. With ten times more competing structures,
folding into ``S_A`` with the same specificity requires a stronger selection (a larger
``\beta``), and the Monte Carlo dynamics relax more slowly. The figure therefore keeps the
sequences with ``P_\text{nat} > 0.98`` (1,555 of 2,000), so the differences with version 1
reflect both the model and this lower stringency.

| statistic | full model vs. reference alignment |
|:--|:--|
| site frequencies ``f_i(v)`` | Pearson ``r = 0.73`` |
| conservation profile (panel d) | Pearson ``r = 0.74`` |
| first principal component (panel e) | ``\lvert\cos\rvert = 0.37`` |
| contact covariance ``C(v, v')`` (panel f) | Pearson ``r = 0.94`` |
| mean ``P_\text{nat}`` | ``0.989`` vs. ``0.996`` |

What changes when all compact structures compete:

- **Negative design is stronger.** The average covariance between cysteines on contacting
  sites doubles (0.17 against 0.09) and the E–K covariance grows (0.10 against 0.06): with
  more competing folds, the sequences rely more on cysteine pairs and salt bridges placed
  on the contacts of ``S_A``. Cysteine becomes frequent at sites 2, 8, 11, 16, 18, 25 and
  27 (panel d).
- **Sites are more conserved** on average (mean conservation score 0.97 against 0.75),
  the most conserved being 24, 1, 27, 20 and 16.
- **The leading principal component is no longer a single charge pattern.** The top three
  eigenvalues of the covariance matrix are 2.3, 1.7 and 1.5, against 1.6, 0.8 and 0.5 for
  the paper's model, so no single direction dominates; PC1 mixes cysteine-pair patterns
  with charges and the projection histogram (g) shows several clusters. Part of this
  clustering is a sampling effect: with 20 chains that rarely leave their basin, each
  cluster also reflects a few chains.

The contact covariance matrix (panel f) keeps the same structure in both versions
(``r = 0.94``): the physics that the paper reads from it, contacts stabilized by cysteine
pairs and by opposite charges, is the same under both models.

Raising the selection strength does not simply restore the paper's stringency. With
``\beta = 3000`` and a burn-in of 50,000 steps (`sample_msa.jl all 20 100 out.txt 3000 50000`),
each chain reaches a stationary ``P_\text{nat}`` within its basin, but the basins differ:
14 of the 20 chains settle at ``P_\text{nat}`` between 0.992 and 0.996, while the other 6
stay trapped between 0.93 and 0.98 for the whole run, and no chain ever leaves its basin.
Overall 16% of the sequences exceed 0.995 (70% exceed 0.99). Under the full model the
sequence landscape at these selection strengths is glassy, and sampling the equilibrium
ensemble at the paper's stringency would require a better sampler (for example many more
chains, or parallel tempering) rather than longer runs.

## Regenerating the figures

The scripts that produced the figures are in `docs/figures/tubiana2019/` and are not run by
the documentation build:

- `sample_msa.jl` samples an alignment (`shakhnovich` or `all` structure set, number of
  chains and of sequences per chain); it uses Julia threads, one chain per thread;
- `analyze_msa.jl` computes the statistics of panels (c)–(g) and the lattice walks of
  ``S_A`` and ``S_G`` from an alignment file;
- `plot_fig4.py` draws the figure with matplotlib and logomaker from those statistics;
- `compare_stats.py` compares the statistics of two alignments.

```bash
julia -t 4 --project=. docs/figures/tubiana2019/sample_msa.jl shakhnovich 4 9000 msa_subset.txt
julia --project=. docs/figures/tubiana2019/analyze_msa.jl msa_subset.txt stats_subset
python docs/figures/tubiana2019/plot_fig4.py stats_subset fig4_subset.png "subset of 10,000 structures"
```

A smaller version of this comparison runs in the test suite (`test/tubiana2019.jl`): it
samples 20 short chains against the subset of 10,000 structures and checks the statistics
of panels (d)–(g) against the reference alignment.
