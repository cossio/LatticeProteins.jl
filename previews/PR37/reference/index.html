Reference

This page documents all symbols provided by LatticeProteins.jl. None of them are exported, so they must be accessed with the LatticeProteins. prefix or imported explicitly (e.g. using LatticeProteins: pnat).

LatticeProteins.LatticeProteins — Module
LatticeProteins

Simulate the lattice proteins model on a 3×3×3 cubic lattice in Julia.

A lattice protein is a simplified model where a protein sequence of 27 amino acids folds on a 3×3×3 cubic lattice. The energy of a sequence in a given structure is determined by pairwise contacts using the Miyazawa-Jernigan interaction matrix. The probability that a sequence folds into a structure is a Boltzmann distribution over the competing compact structures: by default all 103,346 compact structures of the lattice (STRUCTURES), or optionally the representative subset of 10,000 structures of Heo, Maslov & Shakhnovich (2011) used by Jacquin et al. (2016) (SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES).

References

  • Jacquin, Hugo, et al. "Benchmarking inverse statistical approaches for protein structure and design with exactly solvable models." PLoS computational biology 12.5 (2016): e1004889.

Structures and contact maps

LatticeProteins.STRUCTURES — Constant
STRUCTURES

Contact maps of all 103,346 compact structures of a 27-mer on the 3×3×3 cubic lattice, as generated by enumerate_structures. An Array{Int, 3} of size (28, 103346, 2); use STRUCTURES[:, s, :] to get the contact pairs of structure s.

This is the default set of competing structures in pnat, metropolis! and related functions. See SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES for the representative subset of 10,000 structures used by Jacquin et al. (2016).

LatticeProteins.SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES — Constant
SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES

Contact maps of the representative subset of 10,000 compact structures selected by the Shakhnovich group (Heo, Maslov & Shakhnovich, 2011) out of the 103,346 of STRUCTURES, and used as the set of competing structures by Jacquin et al. (2016) and Tubiana et al. (2019). An Array{Int, 3} of size (28, 10000, 2); use SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES[:, s, :] to get the contact pairs of structure s. See load_contact_maps.

Pass structures = SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES to pnat, metropolis! and related functions to compete only against this set, as in those papers and in the Hugo_MSA_pnat values. Structure indices refer to the set in use; use structure_index to translate between the two sets. For example, structure_index(SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES, CONTACT_MAP_A) gives the index (625) of reference structure A in this set.

References

  • Heo, M., Maslov, S., and Shakhnovich, E. "Topology of protein interaction network shapes protein abundances and strengths of their functional and nonspecific interactions." Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 108.10 (2011): 4258-4263.
  • Jacquin, H., et al. "Benchmarking inverse statistical approaches for protein structure and design with exactly solvable models." PLoS Computational Biology 12.5 (2016): e1004889.

LatticeProteins.load_contact_maps — Function
load_contact_maps()

Load the pre-computed contact maps of the representative subset of 10,000 compact structures of Heo, Maslov & Shakhnovich (2011). Returns an Array{Int, 3} of size (28, 10000, 2), where cm[:, s, :] gives the 28 pairs of contacting residue indices of structure s. See SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES.

LatticeProteins.enumerate_structures — Function
enumerate_structures()

Enumerate all maximally compact structures of a 27-mer on the 3×3×3 cubic lattice, that is, all self-avoiding walks visiting the 27 lattice sites, modulo the 48 rotations and reflections of the cube. There are 103,346 of them. The chain direction is kept: reversing the chain maps contact (i, j) to (28 - j, 28 - i), which in general gives a different contact map, so a structure and its chain reversal count as distinct structures (62 of them are their own reversal).

Returns an Array{Int, 3} of size (28, 103346, 2): structures[:, s, :] gives the 28 contact pairs of structure s, as rows (i, j) with i < j, sorted lexicographically. The enumeration order is deterministic. Takes about a second.

The result is available pre-computed as STRUCTURES, which is the default set of competing structures in pnat, metropolis! and related functions; this function is only needed to regenerate it.

References

  • Shakhnovich, E. I., and A. M. Gutin. "Enumeration of all compact conformations of copolymers with random sequence of links." The Journal of Chemical Physics 93.8 (1990): 5967-5971.

LatticeProteins.structure_index — Function
structure_index(structures, cm::AbstractMatrix{Int})
structure_index(structures, cm::Int)

Index within structures of the structure with contact map cm, or nothing if it is not there. structures is an array of structures such as STRUCTURES or SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES. cm is a 28 × 2 matrix of contact pairs, such as STRUCTURES[:, CONTACT_MAP_A, :]; the order of the contacts and the order within each pair are ignored. If cm is an integer, it refers to structure STRUCTURES[:, cm, :] of the default set.

Example

a = structure_index(SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES, CONTACT_MAP_A)  # 625: index of structure A in the reference set
pnat(a, seq; structures = SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES)

Energy and folding probability

LatticeProteins.energy — Function
energy(cm::Int, seq::AbstractVector{Int}; structures = STRUCTURES)

Compute the contact energy of amino acid sequence seq in structure cm.

The energy is the sum of Miyazawa-Jernigan interaction energies over all contacting residue pairs in the given structure. seq must be a vector of length 27 with integer amino acid indices in 1:20. cm is the index of the structure in structures, which defaults to all compact structures STRUCTURES and can be set to the subset SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES.

energies(seq::AbstractVector{Int}; structures = STRUCTURES)

Contact energies of seq in every structure of structures, as a Vector{Float64} of length size(structures, 2). Equivalent to [energy(s, seq; structures) for s in 1:size(structures, 2)], but faster.

LatticeProteins.log_pnat — Function
log_pnat(cm::Int, seq::AbstractVector{Int}; structures = STRUCTURES)

Compute the log-probability that sequence seq folds into structure cm (the "native" structure), as opposed to any of the other structures in structures.

The probability is computed using a Boltzmann distribution at temperature 1 over the structures in structures:

\[\log P_\text{nat}(\text{cm} \mid \text{seq}) = -\log \sum_s \exp\bigl(E(\text{cm}, \text{seq}) - E(s, \text{seq})\bigr)\]

By default structures is STRUCTURES, all 103,346 compact structures of the lattice. Pass structures = SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES to sum only over the representative subset of 10,000 structures used by Jacquin et al. (2016) (see SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES); note that structure indices refer to the set in use.

See also pnat, energy.

Sampling

LatticeProteins.metropolis! — Function
metropolis!(seq::AbstractVector{Int}, cm::Int; β=1, nsteps=1, structures=STRUCTURES)

Perform Metropolis Monte Carlo sampling on the amino acid sequence seq, targeting structure cm. The sequence is mutated in place.

At each step, a random position is selected and mutated to a random amino acid (1–20). The move is accepted or rejected using the Metropolis criterion with inverse temperature β, where the energy is -log_pnat(cm, seq; structures).

The energies of seq in all structures are kept and updated incrementally after each mutation, so a step costs much less than a call to log_pnat. This requires a per-site index of the contacts of structures, which is cached for the default set and rebuilt on every call for any other set; for repeated short runs on another set, prefer random_msa or a single call with a large nsteps.

Arguments

  • seq: integer-coded amino acid sequence of length 27 (values in 1:20).
  • cm: target structure index (in structures).
  • β: inverse temperature (higher values favor sequences that fold into cm).
  • nsteps: number of Monte Carlo steps to perform.
  • structures: set of competing structures, by default all compact structures STRUCTURES. Pass SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES to compete only against the subset of 10,000 structures used by Jacquin et al. (2016) (cm then indexes that set).

Returns the mutated seq.

source
LatticeProteins.random_msa — Function
random_msa(cm::Int; nseqs=1, β=1, nsteps=1000, structures=STRUCTURES)

Generate a random multiple sequence alignment (MSA) of nseqs sequences that fold into structure cm, using Metropolis Monte Carlo sampling.

Each sequence is initialized randomly and then evolved for nsteps Metropolis steps at inverse temperature β. Returns a Matrix{Int} of size (27, nseqs), where each column is an integer-coded amino acid sequence. structures is the set of competing structures (see metropolis!).

See also metropolis!.

LatticeProteins.parallel_tempering — Function
parallel_tempering(cm::Int; betas, nseqs=100, burnin=1000, thin=10,
                   local_steps=10, initial_seqs=nothing, rng=default_rng(),
                   structures=STRUCTURES, threaded=true)

Sample sequences with replica exchange, targeting pnat(cm, seq; structures)^betas[end]. betas must contain at least two finite, nonnegative, strictly increasing inverse selection temperatures (hot to cold). The physical folding temperature stays at 1; all 103,346 compact structures are included by default, just as in log_pnat. Pass structures=SHAKHNOVICH_STRUCTURES for the 10,000-structure subset used in the paper. cm indexes the selected set; use structure_index to translate it.

One round performs local_steps symmetric single-residue proposals per replica, then proposes swaps on alternating disjoint adjacent edges. For two replicas the only edge is attempted every round. A swap is accepted with min(1, exp((betas[i]-betas[j]) * (E[i]-E[j]))), where E = -log_pnat. Cached structural energies are refreshed every 100 rounds to limit roundoff drift.

Discard burnin rounds, then save the coldest sequence after every thin rounds until nseqs samples have been saved. Thus each replica receives (burnin + nseqs * thin) * local_steps mutation proposals. initial_seqs, when provided, is a (27, length(betas)) integer matrix, ordered hot to cold; it is copied. Otherwise each replica starts from an independent uniform sequence. Use an explicit RNG for reproducible runs. Each temperature slot uses its own Xoshiro stream seeded from rng; swaps use rng serially. For fixed arguments and a fixed Julia version, results do not depend on thread count or scheduling. These streams differ from the sampler's original single RNG stream in v2.1.0.

With threaded=true, local updates and cache refreshes run on Julia's default thread pool (start Julia with e.g. --threads=4). Set threaded=false to run serially, for example when parallelizing independent ladders in the caller. Both modes use the same streams and produce identical results.

Returns a named tuple with:

  • samples: coldest-replica MSA, size (27, nseqs). Samples can be correlated.
  • betas: the fixed ladder used in the run.
  • log_pnat, replica_ids: (length(betas), nseqs) traces at saved rounds. Rows follow temperatures; IDs follow sequences through swaps.
  • final_seqs, final_log_pnat: final states at every temperature.
  • burnin_diagnostics, diagnostics: separate burn-in and production counters. local_attempts/local_accepts are indexed by temperature; swap_attempts/swap_accepts by adjacent edge. round_trips is indexed by replica ID and counts completed hot→cold→hot trips entirely within that phase.

Inspect swap acceptance, round trips, and agreement between independent runs. High folding probabilities or accepted swaps alone do not establish equilibrium. Tune the ladder in pilot runs, then hold it fixed for production sampling.

See also metropolis!, random_msa.
